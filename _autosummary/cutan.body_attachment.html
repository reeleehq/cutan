cutan.body_attachment

cutan.body_attachment¶

Props that depict part of a character’s body: the declaration, the geometry, the placement.

A steering wheel with the driver’s hands on it, a sleeve holding a cup, a pair of forearms on a table: art that shows somebody’s limbs, drawn as one prop and laid over a rigged character. Three mistakes reached a viewer in one production, and no check that existed could see any of them:

  1. The art was drawn from the wrong viewpoint. The image model drew the wheel from the driver’s seat (forearms coming from the camera), while the shot looked at the driver from in front, through the windscreen.

  2. The art did not meet the body. Placed by hand, the wheel sat to the right of the driver’s head and was wider than his body, so the arms in it could not come from his shoulders.

  3. The relation drifted between framings. The two-shot and the close-up each placed the wheel by hand, so the driver’s size relative to his own hands changed at the cut.

All three are now one contract, declared ONCE in the character’s own units (its bones) and derived for every framing. The declaration lives in the prop’s document, under metadata.body_attachment (the persisted key BODY_ATTACHMENT_KEY), because what a drawing depicts is a property of the drawing:

{"kind": "PropDescriptor", "name": "wheel_hands", "...": "...",
 "metadata": {"body_attachment": {
     "schema_version": "0.1.0",
     "character": "paul",
     "view": "front",
     "sockets": [
         {"bone": "arm_l", "at": [0.20, 0.45]},
         {"bone": "arm_r", "at": [0.80, 0.45]}
     ]}}}

  • character (optional): the character whose body the art shows, by scene entity id, else store ref. A scene entity binds a reusable prop to another character with overrides: {body_of: jerry} (BODY_OF_OVERRIDE).

  • view: the character’s view the art is drawn in, in the rig’s own words (front, three_quarter, side, back, or any key of the character’s view swap set), or FIRST_PERSON when the camera IS the character (valid only while the character is not on screen).

  • sockets: where each depicted limb meets the body, in the character’s REST view. bone names the character’s bone (arm_l is the bone at the viewer’s left on the factory rig, unmirrored); at is where that bone’s joint would be, in the art’s (u, v) (0..1 of the drawn image, like an attachment anchor). It may lie outside the image: forearms that vanish behind a wheel still say where their shoulders are.

  • scale (optional): the prop’s stage.scale relative to the character’s. Two sockets measure it; one socket needs it.

  • tolerance (default DFLT_TOLERANCE): how far a socket may land from its bone, as a fraction of the span between the sockets’ bones on screen. scale_tolerance (default DFLT_SCALE_TOLERANCE): how far the prop’s size relative to the character may differ from the declared relation.

Unknown keys are kept (forward-compatible): a later field is not “malformed” to an older cutan.

Placement is derived, not authored. A body-attached prop with no stage.at is placed by its body in every shot (the body_placement compile pass, cutan.compile.body), so the relation cannot drift between a two-shot and a close-up. place_body_prop() is the same derivation as a function. cutan.verify.body checks a prop that IS placed by hand against it.

>>> ba = BodyAttachment(character="paul", view="front", sockets=[
...     {"bone": "arm_l", "at": [0.2, 0.45]}, {"bone": "arm_r", "at": [0.8, 0.45]}])
>>> [s.bone for s in ba.sockets], ba.tolerance, ba.scale_tolerance
(['arm_l', 'arm_r'], 0.25, 0.05)

cutan.body_attachment.fit_placement(sockets, targets, *, scale=1.0)[source]¶

The (offset, scale) that best maps sockets onto targets: targets ≈ offset + scale * sockets (least squares, uniform scale, no rotation, since a stage placement has none). With one socket (or sockets that coincide) the scale cannot be measured and scale is kept.

A non-positive scale means the limbs are crossed.

>>> fit_placement([(0, 0), (10, 0)], [(100, 50), (120, 50)])
((100.0, 50.0), 2.0)
>>> fit_placement([(5, 5)], [(100, 50)], scale=3.0)
((85.0, 35.0), 3.0)
>>> fit_placement([(0, 0), (10, 0)], [(120, 50), (100, 50)])[1]
-2.0
Return type:

tuple[tuple[float, float], float]

cutan.body_attachment.place_body_prop(prop_desc, character_desc, *, character_at, character_scale=1.0, art_size)[source]¶

Where to put a body-depicting prop so its sockets meet the character’s bones.

Returns {"at": [x, y], "scale": s, "residual_px": r}: the prop’s stage.at and stage.scale for a character standing at character_at with stage.scale character_scale in its rest view, and the largest distance left between a socket and its bone. The compile pass does this for every prop placed without stage.at; call it for a hand-placed one.

>>> from an.stage.props import PropDescriptor
>>> from cutan.characters.schema import CharacterDescriptor
>>> paul = CharacterDescriptor(name="paul")
>>> wheel = PropDescriptor.model_validate({"name": "w", "slots": [
...     {"name": "body", "bone": "root", "attachment": "w"}],
...     "skins": {"default": {"slots": {"body": {"w": {"path": "parts/w.png"}}}}},
...     "metadata": {"body_attachment": {"character": "paul", "view": "front",
...         "sockets": [{"bone": "arm_l", "at": [0.1, 0.0]},
...                     {"bone": "arm_r", "at": [0.9, 0.0]}]}}})
>>> place_body_prop(wheel, paul, character_at=(200, 400), character_scale=2.0,
...                 art_size=(500, 300))
{'at': [200.0, 270.288], 'scale': 0.9, 'residual_px': 0.0}

The relation is the character’s: at another framing the prop’s scale moves with the character’s, in proportion.

>>> place_body_prop(wheel, paul, character_at=(0, 0), character_scale=3.0,
...                 art_size=(500, 300))["scale"]
1.35

dict[str, Any]

cutan.body_attachment.socket_rig_point(prop_desc, socket, *, art_size)[source]¶

The socket’s at as a point of the prop’s rig (view_box units).

art_size is what the slot’s drawn art rasterises at (pixels for a raster, the SVG’s size), as the compiler’s probe measures it, or a function of the attachment’s path returning it (None: unmeasurable); a declared width/height on the attachment wins, keeping the art’s aspect. Raises NotChecked for what this v1 does not compute: a missing slot or art, unmeasurable art, a nested slot, a posed bone.

>>> from an.stage.props import PropDescriptor
>>> p = PropDescriptor.model_validate({"name": "w", "skins": {"default": {"slots": {
...     "body": {"w": {"path": "parts/w.png"}}}}}, "slots": [
...     {"name": "body", "bone": "root", "attachment": "w"}]})
>>> socket_rig_point(p, Socket(bone="arm_l", at=(0.0, 0.5)), art_size=(400, 200))
(312.0, 512.0)
Return type:

tuple[float, float]
