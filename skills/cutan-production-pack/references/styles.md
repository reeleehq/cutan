# Named styles

A named style is a look the pipeline knows how to set up: frame rate, timing, camera habits, colours, how characters are built, and measured targets a render is checked against. Use one when the writer says "like South Park", "OverSimplified-style" and so on. Never copy a show's characters, logos or voices: a style is a way of moving and composing, not its cast.

How to apply one in a pack:

- **The style pack file:** copy the style's JSON below, exactly, to `assets/styles/<name>.json`, and put its `meta` values (`fps`, `step_hz` if any, `default_easing`) and `style_pack: <name>` in the `scene.md` meta block. Without the file, `style_pack:` is an error.
- **Shots:** lengths inside the style's `shots` range, camera moves only from its `allowed` list, its default transition between shots (a `dissolve` default means a `transition: {kind: dissolve, duration: ...}` on every shot after the first, and a `duration` in meta: see `scene-format.md`).
- **Cast:** build the characters with the style's `characters` knobs: `build`, `head_scale`, `hat`, `hair_style`, `hair_length` and `sash` are `cast.sh` options (`--build`, `--head-scale`, ...; a list means pick one per character); `mouth_variants` is `--mouth-variants`; `palette: per_character` means each character gets its own costume colours. `tint` means `{kind: set, target: <id>, property: tint, value: <colour>, at: 0.0}` on every character in every shot (black makes a silhouette); `part_tints` means the same `set` on each listed part (`<id>/head/left_sclera` to `"#ffffff"` keeps the eyes white inside a black figure); `view` means `{kind: set, target: <id>, property: view, value: <view>, at: 0.0}` in every shot.
- `generate: promote` (Gilliam, Norstein) means the characters are cut from real art, which the factory cannot make: build stand-ins and say so in `PRODUCTION.md`.
- **Motion:** tween lengths inside `tween_duration_s`; `easing` is the style's house curves; `motion_presets` are its signature moves (any preset is allowed).
- **Environment:** a `preset` is a built-in environment `ref`; a `descriptor` is an environment document to write under `assets/environments/`.
- **Guidance lines** describe the look in words. Some name things that are the pipeline owner's, not yours: sound cues and music beds, font files, `flash` or morph transitions, sourcing photographs. Describe those in `PRODUCTION.md` instead of writing them into the pack.
- Tell the writer the **cost class** up front, and list in `PRODUCTION.md` every guidance item the pack cannot express.
- When the script contradicts the style, the script wins: do what the writer wrote, and say in `PRODUCTION.md` which style rule you departed from and where.

### `south_park`: South Park (construction-paper cut-out, digital)

- **Cost:** `low`, rigid shape characters, a locked camera and flat sets; `an character new --offline --build squat --head-scale 1.3 --hat beanie` characters are close to the look.
- **meta:** fps: 24; default_easing: linear; step_hz: 12; style_pack: south_park
- **camera:** default: hold; allowed: hold, push_in
- **shots:** mean_s: 4.2; range_s: 1.5, 9
- **transitions:** default: kind: cut
- **characters:** generate: offline; mouth_variants: happy, sad; build: squat; head_scale: 1.3; hat: beanie, cap, none; sash: False; palette: per_character
- **environment:** preset: indoor
- **easing:** linear, step
- **motion_presets:** waddle, hop, nod, slide_in, slide_out, turn
- **tween_duration_s:** 0.12, 0.25
- *construction:* head, torso and limbs are single rigid pieces; no elbow or knee bends (the squat build is live; the near-circular head with eyes touching is not)
- *eyes:* two white ovals with pupil dots; brows appear when worried or angry; a half-lid per shot
- *views:* the show turns a figure by a hard swap between front and three-quarter heads; that is a plain `set` of the character's `view` (no `turn` play; on twos a near-one-frame turn squashes for two frames and vanishes for one), and the factory's three-quarter body is a stand-in for the show's head-only swap
- *coverage:* two_shot, single_close, over_shoulder
- *motion_vocabulary_unmapped:* whole_piece_rotate
- *voice:* pitch raised; see `live.voice`. The amount is an estimate, and a stock-ffmpeg shift (asetrate + atempo) is a cruder shifter than a studio one
- *sound:* sparse stingers as shot `sounds` cues on the beat they punctuate, no music bed; `an` ships no sound library, so stingers are assets you add with their licence
- *text:* occasional location cards — an overlay text prop (a TextDescriptor on the overlay layer) over the set or a fill plane; the an skill's recipe builds one
